# The Fruit of Gentleness — Sermon Transcript (Cleaned & Organized)

## Opening Prayer

Heavenly Father, we thank You for the many blessings that You have given us in this life, and we pray now that You would give us Your Spirit that we could produce that fruit of praise to You. We ask this in Jesus’ name, Amen.

Please stand and let’s praise God with the song **“People of the River King.”** That song encouraged us to rejoice because of the blessings of Christ, but also because of His invitation to come to Him.

## First Reading: Isaiah 55 — A Gentle Invitation of Grace

Those words in that song are reflective of our first reading from Isaiah 55, in which God issues a gentle invitation to come to Him, and we will note what makes that invitation so acceptable and inviting. It’s an invitation of gentle grace. Here it is from Isaiah 55:

> Come, all you who are thirsty, come to the waters, and you who have no money, come, buy and eat. Come, buy wine and milk without money and without cost. Why spend money on what is not bread, and your labor on what does not satisfy? Listen, listen to me, and eat what is good, and you will delight in the richest of fare. Give ear and come to me; listen, that you may live. I will make an everlasting covenant with you, my faithful love promised to David. See, I have made him a witness to the peoples, a ruler and commander of the peoples. Surely you will summon nations you know not, and nations you do not know will come running to you, because of the Lord your God, the Holy One of Israel, for he has endowed you with splendor. Seek the Lord while he may be found; call on him while he is near. Let the wicked forsake their ways and the unrighteous their thoughts. Let them turn to the Lord, and he will have mercy on them, and to our God, for he will freely pardon.

## Second Reading: Matthew 5 — The Beatitudes

Jesus tells us what the blessed life is like, and He urges us to have an attitude that is gentle in following Him and to live a life of blessing. These are the words we so commonly refer to as the Beatitudes — the blessed attitude of living in the grace of God — from Matthew chapter 5:

> Now when Jesus saw the crowds, he went up on a mountainside and sat down. His disciples came to him, and he began to teach them. He said:
>
> “Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.
> Blessed are those who mourn, for they will be comforted.
> **Blessed are the meek** — and that word “meek” is also the same word in Greek that is sometimes translated _gentle_ — **for they will inherit the earth.**
> Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they will be filled.
> Blessed are the merciful, for they will be shown mercy.
> Blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God.
> Blessed are the peacemakers, for they will be called children of God.
> Blessed are those who are persecuted because of righteousness, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.
> Blessed are you when people insult you, persecute you, and falsely say all kinds of evil against you because of me. Rejoice and be glad, because great is your reward in heaven, for in the same way they persecuted the prophets who were before you.”

This is God’s Word for us: to live in the gentleness of His grace.

Now we’ll profess our faith with the song entitled **“We Believe.”**

## Introduction: Testing Fruit for Ripeness

Before we eat fruit, we like to make sure it’s ripe and ready to eat, that it tastes good. For example, with watermelons, you may always find people thumping them — I’m not quite sure what we’re listening for, but we just do it. Maybe with apples you’ll look to see: is it nice and firm? For a cantaloupe, you might find people squeezing it with both hands — again, I’m not quite sure what we’re looking for, but we do it. And of course, we’re careful — there’s certain fruit items we won’t squeeze, at least not very hard, because we don’t want to break them open or damage them in some way.

But it’s not just the feel, the squeezing that we do. We may also look to see what the color is like. The other day when I was looking at watermelons, there was a lady who asked, “How do we know if it’s good?” And there was another guy standing there, and he said, “Look at the color of the melon. You want to look at that spot where the melon had been resting on the ground — if it’s kind of white or a light yellow, that’s fine. But it’s better if it’s a darker, brighter yellow, because then you know it’s really ripe inside and sweet.” I wasn’t sure if that was true — I didn’t go to the internet and check it out — but sure enough, he was right.

Well, all of that is probably just teaching us that you look at the outside and you get a clue about what’s on the inside, and that might be true with the way we are as people, the way we interact with others. Maybe with some people we have to be kind of forceful and clear about what we want or what our point is. Other times we might just repeat ourselves and end up being frustrating to people. And then, of course, we could be very nice, joyful, and clear in our communication, and people see us as people who are really understanding and helpful.

Well, and that relates to the fruit we want to talk about today — that fruit of the Spirit called **gentleness**. As we continue working down that list of fruit, because Jesus said, being connected to Him, we will bear much fruit in this life, we’re going through that list that the Apostle Paul gave us, and we’re at gentleness now.

Sometimes that word in the Greek is also translated _meekness_, but people have a bit of a misunderstanding about the word “meekness,” because they might think meekness means weakness — that you’re not a very strong person, you’re too easygoing, you’re kind of a pushover, people will walk all over you, you’re going to be misled, or maybe you’re not going to get your way at all. Well, that’s really not what the word meekness means, especially when we see that God is also meek in His dealing with us.

Bible translators like to emphasize that the word “meekness” should really be understood as **“strength that’s under control.”** And when we see how God is meek, or gentle, to us, it’s not meekness at all in the weak sense — rather, we see His strength under His control being used in ways to provide blessings.

So today we want to talk about the fruit of gentleness in our life. But as we have been doing all along with each of these fruits, we’re going to look at how God displays that same characteristic, and it becomes the seed for that fruit in our life. So let’s start out by talking about **the gentleness of God.**

## I. The Gentleness of God

Jesus referred to Himself as being gentle when He said — there’s that invitation again that we commented on earlier:

> “Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For my yoke is easy and my burden is light.”

Jesus displayed that gentleness and meekness when He came into this world as a baby, born in Bethlehem, a little town, and put in a manger. And all through His life, He demonstrated gentleness with people, even when He came to carry out our salvation.

When Mark and Matthew tell us about Jesus entering Jerusalem on Palm Sunday, coming to carry out the work of salvation, they quoted the prophet Zechariah, who told us how the Messiah would come:

> “Say to daughter Zion, ‘See, your king comes to you, gentle and riding on a donkey, on a colt, the foal of a donkey.’”

Jesus came not as some glorious, powerful king with a mighty army following Him, as if He was going to battle against the Jewish leaders or against the Roman government. He came gentle and humble, but that didn’t mean He came without power. No, He came in the power and the will of the Lord to carry out our salvation.

In fact, you see that demonstrated as Jesus was arrested in the Garden of Gethsemane — He didn’t fight against it, but He wasn’t _controlled_ either. He demonstrated that power and healed the man’s ear that had been cut off.

Jesus demonstrated that gentleness when He was put on trial before the governor, and the crowd was calling for His crucifixion, and He was being falsely accused of things — He didn’t open His mouth to speak against them. And when He was being crucified, what did He do? He showed gentleness and prayed for their forgiveness.

Jesus came with power and humbleness because He came to save, and He still does today.

- Jesus came and demonstrated that power to save us when He lived that perfect life against all those temptations the devil threw at Him — He showed His strength and followed God’s will for us.
- Jesus came and demonstrated that power when He taught the truth, so that we would not be deceived and misled by lies.
- He demonstrated that power with His resurrection from the dead, to show He has power over the grave, and therefore we too will rise and have eternal life.

Jesus displayed humbleness and gentleness — strength under control — to accomplish our salvation, and He still shows that today as He calls to us. It’s not some thunderous voice where we snap to and fall like soldiers or robots.

### The Example of Elijah

Let me demonstrate how God calls us. I want to tell you about a situation with the prophet Elijah, who is called “the prophet of fire” because of some of the powerful things that happened and that he did in his ministry. In fact, he had taken on the prophets of Baal, and when he demonstrated that the Lord is the true God and their god Baal was nothing, he had them put to death. Well, that irritated King Ahab, who then put a bounty on his head and said he would chase him down and kill him.

Well, Elijah freaked out, as we would say, and he headed out of the country and went to hide in the desert. And there he sat and had a pity party for himself, under a tree, and then in a cave, because he felt he was the only one left in all of Israel who was faithful to the Lord, and now they’re trying to put him to death.

Well, the Lord comes to him and calls him. Let me show you how:

> “Go out and stand on the mountain in the presence of the Lord, for the Lord is about to pass by.”

What would you expect to see if God were to come?

> Then a great and powerful wind tore the mountains apart and shattered the rocks before the Lord, but the Lord was not in the wind. After the wind there was an earthquake, but the Lord was not in the earthquake. And after the earthquake came a fire — (gee, this sounds like he was in California) — but the Lord was not in the fire. And after the fire came **a gentle whisper.** When Elijah heard it, he pulled his cloak over his face and went out and stood at the mouth of the cave.

That tells us how God calls us — with the gentle whisper of His Word. Oh, we might want to see a demonstration of His power, right? “Come in Your power, Lord, and just straighten out everything that’s messed up with our world, make it right, like we know what’s right.” But instead God simply speaks to us in His Word, as He’s speaking to us now in the Scriptures. That reminds us: listen to the Word, because there God gives us His guidance.

For Elijah, the Lord said, “You go back to Israel — you think you’re the only one there? You’re not.” He had thousands of other believers who were there, and He gave him specific instructions on what Elijah was to do, and what God Himself would do.

It’s a lesson for us: listen to God calling you right here in the Scriptures, where He states clearly what His will is — for us to believe in Him, for us to obey Him — and then look at what He is doing around you in your life, or in the life around you, and see God’s hand at work, and trust it. It’s God’s gentle working, and when you see what He’s doing, you will see His gentle care.

## II. God’s Gentle Care for Us

The prophet Isaiah told us how it is God cares for us. Listen:

> Behold, the Lord God comes with might, and his arm rules for him. Behold, his reward is with him, and his recompense before him.

Again, you’re probably expecting some display of power, but look what he says:

> **He tends his flock like a shepherd. He gathers the lambs in his arms and carries them close to his heart. He gently leads those that have young.**

God comes to us and shows His gentle care first of all by meeting with us. Oh, we can’t see Him, but He’s there. Listen to His promises and see the end result at work.

Jesus used that illustration of Him as a shepherd gathering His sheep in a parable He taught:

> Suppose one of you has a hundred sheep and loses one of them. Doesn’t he leave the ninety-nine in the open country and go after the lost sheep until he finds it? And when he finds it, he joyfully puts it on his shoulders and goes home. Then he calls his friends and neighbors together and says, “Rejoice with me; I have found my lost sheep.” I tell you that in the same way, there will be more rejoicing in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous persons who do not need to repent.

That’s how God takes care of us. When we wander, when we fall, when we find ourselves confused, when we find ourselves in danger — He’s there looking for us. He doesn’t abandon us. He doesn’t say, “Oh well, that’s just one.” No, He goes after us. He calls us with His Word. He puts us in His arms, on His shoulders, and He rejoices over us being found.

In fact, the Bible tells us **He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds.** When your heart is broken, when you’re sad, when you’re confused, just feel the loving arms of God around you. He is with us. It’s what He said. It’s what is true.

He does it in order to lead us. He knows where He wants us to be, and He will lead us there, gently, with His Word. Just look and see how God has led you to His pastures, to His fields of blessing.

Isn’t it wonderful, the way God gently works in our life — to come to us when we have wandered from Him, to call us not with threats but with His loving invitation of forgiveness and grace, to guide us step by step all through life, to care for us, to bind our broken hearts and to heal our wounds.

Do you see that in your life? Maybe we don’t as often as we should. Maybe we take all of these things just for granted — “That’s just the way life is.” Or maybe we take credit for it — “Look at what I’ve done to make my life good.” But when there’s trouble or hardship in our life, oh, then we say, “Where is the Lord? Where is His blessing? Why is He so harsh with me?”

### The Example of Job

Remember the Old Testament believer Job, who was blessed by God in so many ways, but then God allowed all that to be taken away and to cause some personal suffering in his own life, and he thought God was just being unfair and too harsh with him. So one of his friends comes and says:

> “Are God’s consolations not enough for you — words spoken **gently** to you?”

Don’t live by sight — live by faith in those gentle and sure promises of God. That means we need to trust Him, because He does what He says.

You’re that sheep that has wandered. You’re the sheep that’s scared. You’re the sheep that needs some guidance. He’s looking for you. And when you have fallen, when you have strayed from His will, He is there to pick you up and to hug you with forgiveness and grace, because He redeemed you with His blood. You are His forever.

That’s the gentleness of God that we experience, and by the Spirit of God we can believe it and see it, and by the Spirit of God, that seed of gentleness that He shows us can become fruit in our lives — all focused on what Christ has done for us.

## III. Pursuing the Fruit of Gentleness

That’s why the Apostle Paul said:

> “But you, man of God, flee from all this…”

He was just referring to greed and a love for money and worldly things. He had just talked about what’s true for all of us — there are different degrees of greed, or want for things in life. We all have needs — things that we need for this life, and so we want them. And then we go to the next degree: we want life to be easier and more comfortable. And then the next degree: we want things to be really fun and full of all sorts of pleasures. And then the next degree — this is the ultimate — “I want _that_,” and we forsake God.

Paul was talking about people who go to that degree, then forsake their faith and suffer all kinds of things. So he says, “You, man of God, flee from all that, and instead pursue **righteousness, godliness, faith, love, endurance, and gentleness.**”

All of those fruits that we have been talking about — from those will flow the fruit of gentleness. So let’s talk about our pursuit of gentleness, how we can show it in our life. There are two dimensions to it.

### (1) Gentleness Toward God — Shown in Our Character

This is marked by faith, by trust, and by following Him. The Apostle James reminds us:

> “Humbly accept the word planted in you, which can save you.”

That’s where faith will come from — listen to the Word and let your faith and gentleness grow.

King Solomon wrote this in Ecclesiastes:

> “Guard your steps when you go to the house of God. Go near to listen rather than to offer the sacrifice of fools, who do not know that they do wrong.”

The sacrifice of fools is when we open our mouths and tell God how things should be, rather than going and listening to Him tell us who He is and what He does — and we call that worship.

Show the gentleness of your character to God — trusting Him, following Him — and then thanking Him with your worship. From that gentleness of our character toward God will come the gentleness of our **conduct** toward others.

Paul said:

> “Let your gentleness be evident to all, for the Lord is near.”

### (2) Gentleness Toward Others — Shown in Our Conduct

The Apostle Paul gave us a beautiful description of this:

> “Brothers and sisters, if someone is caught in a sin, you who live by the Spirit should restore that person gently. But watch yourselves, or you also may be tempted. Carry each other’s burdens, and in this way you will fulfill the law of Christ.”

He’s urging us to show gentleness by helping the weak and the weary. By “the weak,” I mean those who are spiritually weak — those who have been caught in a sin. He used a word that described an animal being caught in a trap — it didn’t know it was there, or it would have avoided it. Doesn’t that happen to us sometimes, where we’re not paying attention, we’re not careful, and then we find ourselves caught in a trap?

He tells us, “Restore that person gently.” Now, the word he used for “restore” was a medical term used to describe a doctor setting a broken bone. How do you think a doctor sets a broken bone? Do they just push it back together? No — mindful of the pain you’re suffering, they are gentle to put that bone back together for you.

In the same way, though we are spiritual and see that somebody has done something wrong, we should be gentle to restore them using the forgiveness and grace of God — not a know-it-all attitude: “Oh, that’s not how you do it, I don’t do that, we don’t do that.” But speaking words of forgiveness.

In fact, Paul warned: “Watch yourselves, or you also may be tempted” — be careful that you don’t get caught in that same trap.

Jesus said something similar:

> “Why do you look at the speck of sawdust in your brother’s eye and pay no attention to the plank in your own eye? How can you say to your brother, ‘Let me take the speck out of your eye,’ when all the time there is a plank in your own eye? You hypocrite, first take the plank out of your own eye, and then you will see clearly to remove the speck from your brother’s eye.”

That’s gentleness — understanding the weakness and the wound that we feel from our own sin, so that we can restore somebody else with the forgiveness of Christ. And that means pastors have to do it too. The writer to the Hebrews said:

> “The high priest is able to deal gently with those who are ignorant and going astray, since he himself is subject to weakness. That is why he has to offer sacrifice for his own sins, as well as for the sins of the people.”

I have to admit and confess my own sins too, in order to help you, so that we can all know the forgiving grace of God.

### Not Repaying Evil, But Overcoming It with Good

So Paul tells us to do just the opposite thing — do not repay evil for evil:

> Be careful to do what is right in the eyes of everyone. If it is possible, as far as it depends on you, live at peace with everyone. Do not take revenge, my dear friends, but leave room for God’s wrath, for it is written: “It is mine to avenge; I will repay,” says the Lord. On the contrary: “If your enemy is hungry, feed him; if he is thirsty, give him something to drink. In doing this, you will heap burning coals on his head.” Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good.

Paul also said, “Carry one another’s burdens” — and here he’s referring to those who are weak, like Jesus said, who are weary and heavy-laden. And you see somebody who has some difficulties, some hardships in life, and you’re willing to offer some help. Maybe it’s just a kind word or a smile. For sure, it can be your prayers given to the Lord for that person. That’s how we show gentleness to others.

### Gentleness in Witnessing the Truth

But another way is when we are witnessing the truth to others, especially those who don’t understand or who might be opposed to the truth. Listen how Peter urges us to do it:

> “But in your hearts” — that’s where it starts — “revere Christ as Lord. Always be prepared to give an answer to everyone who asks you to give the reason for the hope that you have. But do this with gentleness and respect.”

With gentleness — you’re not out to win an argument, you’re out to share the truth for their salvation.

When our high school took their trip in May down to Disneyland, as we were walking to the entrance gates, there were two guys standing there with these — they went up probably ten feet — loaded with posters, and each of them had a bullhorn, and they were telling us how sinful we were and how we needed to repent or we’re going to hell. And I thought, is this really a respectful, holy message? There are families who are wanting to go to Disneyland and have some fun, and instead you’re putting them down, and you don’t even know them. That was not done with gentleness and respect. That was not a message of hope.

Let us share the message of hope of God’s love in Jesus. As we witness with the Word, remember:

> “A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger.”

And with opponents:

> “They must be gently instructed, in the hope that God will grant them repentance, leading them to a knowledge of the truth.”

There’s the goal — that’s the purpose of our teaching and our response: that people know the truth, repent of their sin, and come to know the forgiveness of God in Jesus.

---

## Conclusion

Gentleness. Don’t overlook God’s gentleness when it’s given to you, and don’t ignore using it when it’s needed by others. And you know, just a little bit goes a long way. If you want to add a little flavor to your water, you just squeeze a little lemon juice in it. If you want something refreshing, you can just grab a few grapes — you don’t need the whole bunch at one time.

**A little bit of gentleness can go a long way. Learn it from Jesus. Amen.**
