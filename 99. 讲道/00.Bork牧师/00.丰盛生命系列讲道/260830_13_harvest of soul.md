
# Sermon: The Harvest of the Fruit of Souls

## Opening Prayer

We pray now that you’d open our eyes, that we can see the harvest of souls that is around us, and praise you, and work for you to bring those souls into your kingdom. We ask for this blessing in Jesus’ name, Amen.

## Introduction: Summer Growing, Fall Harvest

Summer season, as those gardens are growing, it was a good opportunity for us to see how the Holy Spirit grows our faith and those Christian blessings that come with it. But we’re going to make a little bit of a shift now. We’re not done talking about those fruits, but we’re going to shift a little bit in our attention about them — it’s not so much what’s growing in us, but what do we do with the growth that we see? And today, it’s about the growth we see in others, and that kind of fits in with the season of the year. Summer is the growing season, but fall is the harvest season, so today we’re going to talk about the harvest of fruit of souls.

## Naaman’s Story (2 Kings 5)

There was a captive from the nation of Israel who was working as a servant in the household of a commander who had leprosy. She simply shared the suggestion with her mistress that if he only knew there was a prophet of God in Israel, he could be cured of his leprosy. That was told to this commander, and he went to Israel, but the prophet simply sent him a message and said, “Go wash in the Jordan River.” He was a little upset by that because he thought there’d be more of a show — something spectacular that would have to be done. So he was a bit angry, thinking he was being put off. But he wasn’t. He was encouraged then to just follow the directions given to him, and so he washed in the Jordan River, and he was cleansed of his leprosy. But more than that, he came to know the true God.

The point for us is simply this: it was because of the invitation given by a little girl that he learned about the message of the true God. So for you and I, it’s simply sharing that message — that invitation to come and learn about the true God — and people will come to faith.

Listen to this account from Second Kings, chapter five:

> Now Naaman was commander of the army of the king of Aram. He was a great man in the sight of his master and highly regarded, because through him the Lord had given victory to Aram. He was a valiant soldier, but he had leprosy.
>
> Now bands of raiders from Aram had gone out and taken captive a young girl from Israel, and she served Naaman’s wife. She said to her mistress, “If only my master would see the prophet who is in Samaria, he would cure him of his leprosy.”
>
> Naaman went to his master and told him what the girl from Israel had said. “By all means, go,” the king of Aram replied. “I will send a letter to the king of Israel.” So Naaman left, taking with him ten talents of silver, six thousand shekels of gold, and ten sets of clothing — he thought he needed to pay something to hear about or to get a blessing from God. Not true.
>
> The letter that he took to the king of Israel read, “With this letter, I am sending my servant Naaman to you, so that you may cure him of his leprosy.” As soon as the king of Israel read the letter, he tore his robes and said, “Am I God? Can I kill and bring back to life? Why does this fellow send someone to me to be cured of his leprosy? See how he’s trying to pick a quarrel with me.”
>
> When Elisha, the man of God, heard that the king of Israel had torn his robes, he sent him this message: “Why have you torn your robes? Have the man come to me, and he will know that there is a prophet in Israel.” So Naaman went with his horses and chariots and stopped at the door of Elisha’s house. Elisha sent a messenger to him to say, “Go wash yourself seven times in the Jordan River, and your flesh will be restored, and you will be cleansed.”
>
> But Naaman went away angry, and said, “I thought that he would surely come out to me and stand and call on the name of the Lord his God, wave his hand over the spot and cure me of my leprosy. Are not Abana and Pharpar, the rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel? Couldn’t I wash in them and be cleansed?” So he turned and went off in a rage.
>
> Naaman’s servants went to him and said, “My father, if the prophet had told you to do some great thing, would you not have done it? How much more then, when he tells you, ‘Wash and be cleansed’?” So he went down and dipped himself in the Jordan seven times, as the man of God had told him, and his flesh was restored and became clean like that of a young boy.
>
> Then Naaman and all his attendants went back to the man of God. He stood before him and said, “Now I know that there is no God in all the world except in Israel.”

You see what a simple invitation could do. And so it is that you and I have that privilege to simply share with others the message about Jesus.

## Sermon Body: Harvesting the Fruit of Souls

As I mentioned at the start of our service, our summer series of messages has been about living a fruitful life — because when we’re connected with Jesus by our faith in Him, He said we will produce much fruit. We’ve spent the past several weeks looking at the different fruit that God’s Spirit produces in our life, and I know a number of you have said this has been very helpful and encouraging to you in your walk with God.

We’re not quite done talking about the different fruits the Spirit produces, but our focus shifts a little bit. It’s not just what He’s going to do or has been doing in us, but also what He is going to do _through_ us — and that is harvest the fruit of souls. You might think, “Well, I guess we’re not going to be talking about me so much.” That isn’t true, because we’re going to see exactly how the Spirit has been working in our life to produce faith in us, how He has harvested us, how He has gathered us together, and now, how He is going to use us to harvest the fruit of the souls of others.

### The Woman at the Well (John 4)

An excellent example of how that is done was an incident in Jesus’ life when He was waiting next to a well, and a woman came to draw water. Jesus began to talk with her and offered her some water. She noticed Jesus didn’t have anything with which he could draw water from the well, but Jesus told her that the water he was offering was living water. He then began to talk to her about her life, and she began to recognize her need for a Savior. Jesus revealed that He was that Savior.

Look at how the conversation continued, from John chapter four:

> Then, leaving her water jar, the woman went back to the town and said to the people there, “Come, see a man who told me everything I ever did. Could this be the Messiah?” They came out of the town and made their way toward him.
>
> Meanwhile, Jesus’ disciples urged him, “Rabbi, eat something.” But he said to them, “I have food to eat that you know nothing about.” Then his disciples said to each other, “Could someone have brought him food?”
>
> “My food,” said Jesus, “is to do the will of him who sent me and to finish his work. Don’t you have a saying, ‘It’s still four months until the harvest’? I tell you, open your eyes and look at the fields! They are ripe for harvest. Even now the one who reaps draws a wage and harvests a crop for eternal life, so that the sower and the reaper may be glad together. Thus the saying ‘One sows and another reaps’ is true. I sent you to reap what you have not worked for. Others have done the hard work, and you have reaped the benefits of their labor.”
>
> Now many of the Samaritans from that town believed in him because of the woman’s testimony, “He told me everything I ever did.” So when the Samaritans came to him, they urged him to stay with them, and he stayed two days. And because of his words many more became believers. They said to the woman, “We no longer believe just because of what you said; now we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this man really is the Savior of the world.”

And that’s how the harvest of souls takes place.

### The Apple Tree (Personal Story)

When I was a kid, we had a big apple tree in our backyard, and we always looked forward to eating those apples. But of course, we had to wait until they were ripe. We’d see them turn red, and once in a while we’d steal an apple. But my dad would always tell us, “No, leave the apples there. We don’t pick them until the first frost of the season.” I don’t know scientifically everything that happens, but when that frost came, it just put an extra zip, so to speak, into the taste of that apple.

So as soon as that first frost came, Dad would gather us kids together, and he’d put backpacks on us backwards — that is, on our front — and send us up the trees to pick those apples. We’d pick them and put them right into that back sack, then go down and empty them carefully into a bushel basket so we didn’t bruise the apples. There my mother would sort them out — some good for baking, some good for canning (she’d make a lot of applesauce), and some good for just eating right there on the spot, nice and raw. That event was really a big family event. Everyone got together to do the harvesting.

Now I don’t have any fruit trees in my backyard, but I really like homegrown fresh tomatoes, though I’ve never done well growing them. So this summer I said I’m going to do that — but I cheated. I went to the store and bought full-grown plants that already had tomatoes hanging on them. Now I’ve been waiting about five weeks for those tomatoes to ripen. They’re taking a long time, but they’re there. I guess I need to work on that fruit-ification that the Spirit gives us — there will be a harvest.

## The Stages of Growing Faith (Mark 4)

There’s a process that takes place, and Jesus talked about the steps or phases of the growing of faith. It’s important for us to recognize those stages. Let’s take a look at this parable Jesus told:

> This is what the kingdom of God is like. A man scatters seed on the ground. Night and day, whether he sleeps or gets up, the seed sprouts and grows, though he does not know how. All by itself the soil produces grain — first the stalk, then the head, then the full kernel in the head. As soon as the grain is ripe, he puts the sickle to it, because the harvest has come.

With that, Jesus is reminding us of the stages of growing faith.

### Stage One: Scattering and Planting the Seed

Of course, Jesus wasn’t talking about growing apple trees or tomatoes — He’s talking about growing faith. So the seed that gets scattered is the Word of God. We know from gardening, and just like farmers with their fields, you have to prepare the ground first — and that’s true with the human heart. It has to be prepared to receive that Word and let it grow.

Why is that? Because the human heart is hard sometimes. It has obstacles that keep that Word from growing, that keep people from simply believing. It might mean people are just turned off by religion, or think it’s all fantasy stuff and not true, or maybe they’ve been hurt in some way by religion in the past. Maybe religion presents a challenge to them, and they don’t like the message they hear — either because it’s hard for them to believe, or because it makes some demand on their life that they’re not ready for. Or maybe they’re just going through a tough time, maybe they’ve had some troubles in their life, and they just can’t see how God is a loving God who would allow that difficulty into their life.

Maybe we relate to some of those things too — whether it’s challenges we have faced, or the difficulty of really believing and holding on to something that’s hard to believe. So in preparing that ground, look at your own experiences and understand what humans might be going through, and then you’re ready to do some harvesting too. In other words, you can turn small talk into big talk. You can tell them about a God who knows exactly what you’re going through. In fact, Jesus went through a lot of those same issues. You can tell them about a God who not only knows but cares, and who can show you and help you through all things. Best of all, you can show them that they have a relationship with God because of Jesus.

We have what people need, whatever their need is. We have God, and they need God. So the next step after preparing the ground is to simply share the Word about God. That’s exactly what Jesus did with the woman who came looking for water. He started with some small talk, but turned it into big talk as he focused her on her need for forgiveness. She had been living with a lot of guys, and Jesus was pointing out to her her sin and the need for a Savior. She believed a Savior would come one day, and Jesus revealed Himself as that Savior.

The Apostle Peter told us we don’t have to have some big theological discourse to present to people. Let’s simply do this:

> But in your hearts revere Christ as Lord, and always be prepared to give an answer to everyone who asks you to give the reason for the hope that you have. But do this with gentleness and respect. (1 Peter 3:15)

In other words, simply share the hope, the joy, the comfort you have from knowing about God and His love for you.

### Stage Two: Nurturing the Word with Grace

After we scatter and plant that Word, the second stage is to nurture that Word — to nurture the faith that’s starting to grow, and to do so with grace. The Apostle Paul talked about that. Just like when we do gardening, we have to water the plants and make sure there’s sunshine. So we have to look at the soul and its need too for grace. Paul talked about this describing his ministry in Corinth:

> I planted the seed, Apollos watered it, but God has been making it grow. So neither he who plants nor he who waters is anything, but only God, who makes things grow. The one who plants and the one who waters have one purpose, and they will each be rewarded according to their own labor. For we are co-workers in God’s service; you are God’s field, God’s building. (1 Corinthians 3:6-9)

So after the seed is planted, we need to nurture that faith with the water of God’s grace.

Sometimes people are turned off by religion or by the church because all they hear is a set of rules people have to follow and live by, and that’s what they think it means to be religious or to have faith — and they’re not ready for that. They’re not ready to have their life all changed, to give up things they enjoy doing. But that’s not what saves us. It’s not what we do, but what God has done. That’s grace.

Look at what God has done for you in Jesus. Then we need to make sure we’re clear: yes, our life has changed, but that’s my response to what God has done to change my life. I’m not trying to earn anything from God. I’m trying to give Him my thanks, and to know that when I live His way, I am blessed.

### Stage Three: Giving It Light

Then we need to give that faith some light, like plants need sunlight. Give them the light of the gospel. Let them know what God has done for them: that though we were sinful and separated from God, God came to bring us back to Him. He did that through Jesus, His Son, coming into this world to live a life that you and I are impossible to live — that is, to live sinlessly. Jesus did that for us; His sinlessness becomes ours simply by believing in Him. God credits it to us.

Then Jesus took upon Himself our healing. He suffered the wrath of God for the sins of the whole world, so that you and I would never experience that wrath. Instead, we have God’s forgiveness and love, proven to us as Jesus was raised from the dead. That is the proof that the penalty, the curse for sin, has ended. Jesus came back to life, and He promises that we too will live. Now Jesus lives in heaven and rules over all things — all things — for our good, for our blessing. That’s the message of the gospel. That’s the light that shines on people and grows their faith.

## Trusting the Spirit and Prayer

Now, we may be a little concerned that maybe we’re not going to say the right thing, or know exactly what we should say. But we also have to use the power of prayer when we talk to people, recognizing that it’s God who’s going to be doing the work. Jesus even told his disciples:

> Don’t worry about what you’re going to say. Just say whatever is given to you at the time, for it’s not you speaking, but the Holy Spirit.

Recognize that it’s God’s word that you share, and therefore God will work through that Word to bring faith. So after you have prepared that ground, made that connection, and shared the gospel with people, watch how God’s Spirit goes to work, creating understanding in the hearts of people.

The Bible tells us that on our own we can’t understand the things of the Spirit of God — they’re too hard for us to understand, and sometimes we don’t want to understand or accept them. But the Spirit creates that understanding and that faith in us, and gets us to accept them as truth.

Having faith in a God who loves and forgives and blesses, your life is filled with joy, assurance, and comfort that His promises are real, and you are blessed. That’s the growing of faith. That’s the work of the Spirit to give us that fruit of faith — and we’ve all experienced that.

## The Starfish Story

There’s a little story about two guys walking along the beach who noticed that a lot of starfish had been washed up onto the shore. One guy bent over and started picking those starfish up and throwing them back in the water. His friend, looking at the hundreds of starfish there, said, “You’re not going to be able to save all of these — so why are you doing it?” And he responded, “Because it matters to this one.”

There are a lot of people out there who don’t know about God, who don’t have faith in God. They all need God, and we have God’s call to share with them the good news of Jesus, because it matters to them. It matters to you.

So prepare yourself, and see how you have been given that gift of faith in God, and hold to that faith — because you never know what may happen in your life. From what we’ve been hearing in the news these last few days — the tragedy in Nepal, where out of nowhere it seems this huge flood of water comes down the mountain and wipes out towns. Thousands are still missing; hundreds have been found dead. I heard scientists had warned this could happen — I don’t know what you can do if a glacier is going to move, but maybe they should have been living somewhere else. I don’t know. But the point is this: you never know when disaster may come into your life. Be prepared with faith in God that saves you from this life and gives you eternal life — and it’s not just you, but the thousands out there.

## The Harvest Is Ready: See Them, Reach Them, Keep Them

God has prepared a harvest. It is ready. Now He calls us to work.

When Jesus met up again with his disciples, they were just ready to have a nice lunch with him. But Jesus told them, “My food is to do the will of Him who sent me, and to finish His work. Don’t you have a saying, ‘It’s still four months until harvest’? Well, look, I tell you, open your eyes and look at the fields — they are ripe for harvest.” I would imagine Jesus was pointing down the valley where they saw hundreds of people coming out of that town to talk to him.

So Jesus says to us: “See them, for the harvest is plentiful.” They’re all around us.

### See Them

Look at your families. Parents, look at your children — they need to know Jesus. Praise them to the Lord. If you’re empty nesters, you still have children, and they still need to know the Lord. If you’ve never been blessed with children, you have parents, and they need to know the Lord. Perhaps you have siblings, other relatives, neighbors, co-workers, this community. There are people all around us.

In fact, in two weeks — Sunday, September 13th — the city is shutting down this street for five hours, so that hundreds, maybe thousands, of people can walk through South San Jose, and they’re going to be walking right past our church. Jesus said, “Wide is the street that leads to destruction, and many are on it.” There may be people walking past us who don’t know or don’t care about Jesus, but you know what? They need Him, and we have Him.

So we have the opportunity to get out from behind these walls that morning and be out here presenting God’s Word. Our wonderful band is going to provide some music as people walk by. We can have volunteers out there to hand out water or snacks, share some information about our church, invite them to come sit in the shade for a little bit. Whatever it might be, we have the opportunity to share with them. The harvest is plentiful, but Jesus tells us workers are needed.

So when you hear and see all the things we do at this church and our schools to share God’s Word, ask yourself: Is there something I could be doing? Then ask somebody who’s doing the work: Is there something I could do? And then step up and do it.

### Reach Them

See them, and then reach them. Look what the woman at the well did. She left her water jar and went back to town and said to the people, “Come and see the man who told me everything I ever did. Could this be the Messiah?” And they came out of the town and made their way toward him.

When you see them, reach out to them, invite them. She simply said, “Come and see.” Come and hear what I hear and what I know, because they need to know it too. Then engage them with the truth. You don’t need a big theological discourse. You simply need, as Peter said, to share the reason for the hope that you have — Jesus. “Let me tell you about my God.” And look what happened: many of the Samaritans from that town believed in him because of the woman’s testimony, “He told me everything I ever did.” So the Samaritans came to him, urged him to stay with them, and he stayed two days. And because of his words, many more became believers. They said to the woman, “We no longer believe just because of what you said; now we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this man really is the Savior of the world.”

### Keep Them

After we see them and reach out to them, there’s one more thing we need to do: keep them with the Lord. Keep them connected to God’s Word, connect them to the worship of God, connect them to God’s people, serve them according to their needs, and then invite them to serve others. That’s our calling.

There’s a story of a young man applying for a job as an usher at a movie theater. In the interview, he was asked, “What would you do if there was a fire?” He said, “Well, don’t worry about me — I know how to get out of this place.” But that’s not his job as an usher. It’s to show _others_ how to get out of that place. We know the way, but others may not. It’s our calling to share with them the way, the truth, and the life — to make a difference in somebody’s life by giving them Jesus.

So let’s join that harvest of the fruit of souls. Amen.
