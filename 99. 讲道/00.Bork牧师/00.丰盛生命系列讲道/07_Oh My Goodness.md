## Part One: Closing Scripture and Affirmation of Faith

[The speaker is explaining where the Thessalonians' faith and love came from]

He notes where it comes from — the faith and the love that was instilled in them by God — and how they were now showing that faith and love to others, all to the glory of God. So as we talk about living the good life today, we are reminded that this goodness is simply a reflection of God's goodness to us.

Here are Paul's words in his second letter to the Thessalonians, from chapter one:

> Grace and peace to you from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. We ought always to thank God for you, brothers and sisters, and rightly so, because your faith is growing more and more, and the love all of you have for one another is increasing. Therefore, among God's churches we boast about your perseverance and faith in all the persecutions and trials you are enduring. All this is evidence that God's judgment is right, and as a result you will be counted worthy of the kingdom of God, for which you are suffering... We constantly pray for you, that our God may make you worthy of his calling, and that by his power he may bring to fruition your every desire for goodness and your every deed prompted by faith. We pray this so that the name of our Lord Jesus may be glorified in you, and you in him, according to the grace of our God and the Lord Jesus Christ.

So with these words, Paul is reminding us that we want to live in a way that reflects the goodness of God. In order to do that, we need to pray for God's blessing, for his goodness.

Jesus also taught us about the goodness of God, giving us one example of it in our life — that God hears and answers our prayers, always in good ways. Jesus' words are recorded by Luke in chapter 11 of his Gospel:

> So I say to you: Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and the door will be opened to you. For everyone who asks receives; the one who seeks finds; and to the one who knocks, the door will be opened. Which of you fathers, if your son asks for a fish, will give him a snake instead? Or if he asks for an egg, will give him a scorpion? If you then, though you are evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him!

And so it is that God displays his goodness to us as he hears and answers all of our prayers.

Now let's profess our faith in this God who shows his goodness to us, with a statement entitled "I Will Walk with the Lord." Together we say:

> I will walk with the Lord, for it is he who has given me life.
> 
> Heavenly Father, you created my inmost being; you knit me together in my mother's womb. I praise you because I am fearfully and wonderfully made; your works are wonderful, I know that full well.
> 
> I will walk with the Lord, for it is he who has saved me. Loving Savior, I know that it was not with perishable things such as silver or gold that you redeemed me from the empty way of life handed down to me from my ancestors, but with the precious blood of Christ, a lamb without blemish or defect. He was chosen before the creation of the world, but was revealed in these last times for my sake. Through him I believe in God, who raised him from the dead and glorified him, and so my faith and hope are in God.
> 
> I will walk with the Lord, for it is he who is sanctifying me. Holy Spirit, when the set time had fully come, God sent his Son, born of a woman, born under the law, to redeem those under the law, that I might receive adoption to sonship. Because I am his child, God has sent the Spirit of his Son into my heart, the Spirit who calls out, "Abba, Father." So I am no longer a slave, but God's child; and since I am his child, God has made me also an heir.
> 
> May God bless us in that walk.

[Congregation stands to sing]

## Part Two: Sermon — "Oh My Goodness"

(On the fruit of the Spirit: goodness / Galatians 5)

Wow! Wow!

An article I read went on to say — maybe it's the way we present things that makes people just hear "blah, blah, blah, we've heard this before." Maybe it's too many familiar things, or maybe we're using technical language, new terms you haven't heard before, and it's just too hard to follow, so you tune out. All along we're trying to "wow" you. So today it's my attempt to try to wow you — I've entitled the message _Oh My Goodness_. That's probably a phrase you've heard before; maybe you've said it yourself. People say that when they've been surprised by something — maybe something good, maybe something bad. Either way: oh my goodness!

Now you may be wondering where I'm going with this, because we're supposed to be talking about living a fruitful life. That's what Jesus said — being connected to him by faith, we will produce much fruit. Well, Paul, in his list of the fruits of the Spirit, listed goodness. So today we're going to talk about that fruit of goodness in our life, with that thought: Oh my goodness! Yes, we're going to talk about living a life of goodness.

But as we've focused on with all of the different fruits on that list, we all start with God. Just like when you eat a piece of fruit — that fruit grew from a tree, which grew from a seed — so we have to go to the seed to see where this goodness comes from. So let's take a look at God's goodness.

### 1. What Is God's Goodness?

Maybe we need to define it first. What do we mean by "goodness"? We may have different definitions of what's good, just like you might say a fruit is good or pleasing to you, and we all have different tastes about that. We might refer to people as being good because they're pleasant, or because they do good things — we compare them to people who do bad things, and that's how we arrive at our definition of goodness.

Well, why don't we let God tell us what that goodness is? In 2 Peter chapter 1 we hear this:

> His divine power has given us everything we need for a godly life through our knowledge of him who called us by his own glory and goodness.

[Note: the recording cuts off here. The sense of the following, missing sentence appears to be that when God talks about the goodness we are to live, he's talking about that obedience — the perfect obedience we are to have to all of his laws.]

Jesus pointed that out early in his ministry when he said this:

> For truly I tell you, until heaven and earth disappear, not the smallest letter, not the least stroke of a pen, will by any means disappear from the Law until everything is accomplished.

Sometimes people today will say, "Well, those laws, those things in the Bible — that was for a different time, a different era. It's changed now." That's not what Jesus said. He said not until heaven and earth disappear, not until the end of time, will his laws disappear.

He goes on:

> Therefore anyone who sets aside one of the least of these commands and teaches others accordingly will be called least in the kingdom of heaven, but whoever practices and teaches these commands will be called great in the kingdom of heaven. For I tell you that unless your righteousness surpasses that of the Pharisees and the teachers of the law, you will certainly not enter the kingdom of heaven.

Pretty strong words. Jesus was giving a comparison here with the Pharisees and the teachers of the law — Jewish teachers who were very insistent on absolute obedience to all the laws, every point of the law of God. In fact they kind of bragged and boasted about their obedience. They were so serious about people obeying the laws of God that they even came up with 630 additional laws to help people keep the laws of God.

Now, Jesus wasn't commending them for that — in fact he often pointed out their hypocrisy. But what he was trying to emphasize is that we need that perfect obedience to be in the presence of our holy God.

Sometimes we might think, "Well, I know I can't be perfect, but I'm pretty good — I'm good enough." But good enough in our eyes is not good enough in God's eyes, who said, "You must be perfect, as your Father in heaven is perfect." You can see the trouble we're in: that righteousness, that goodness of God, is something he requires of us, and we can't do it.

I know we probably think we're all pretty good people, right? We're a church; we're not in jail. We'd probably say, "Sure, I'm not perfect, but I'm not like those guys down the street doing this or that — I'm not robbing, I'm not hurting people." But God said we have to be absolutely perfect, and we can't. And there's a reason for that: it's because of who we are — because of our sin nature.

### 2. A Little Story: The Dirt Mound and the Flower Bed

Let me illustrate with a little story. This summer I've been trying to make my backyard better with flowers and things. I've tried before, actually — several years ago, when we were doing work on the patio, the guys digging up the ground came up with a lot of dirt and didn't know what to do with it. I said, "I'll take the dirt" — I was going to build a landscape in my backyard, a big mound of dirt in the corner with grass and a lot of plants. It would look beautiful.

Well, I have three little boys in the house, and they had different plans, because to them a pile of dirt wasn't a landscape project — it was a playground. Within a week that mound of dirt was flattened out, and I'd say it lost some of its volume too, since it ended up on their clothes, in their pockets, in their shoes. That didn't work, because they saw it for a different purpose.

A few years later, they weren't little boys anymore, so I thought I'd try something else. I removed some big bushes from the backyard and put in a flower bed along the fence. Problem is, those boys are now playing basketball, and sometimes they'd miss the hoop or not get the rebound, and the ball would go into the flower bed and flatten all the flowers.

Now, what am I trying to illustrate? Nothing about little boys — about our nature. One, sometimes we just want to do something fun, even though that's what we're not supposed to do. And sometimes we just aren't able to do what we're supposed to do — like they couldn't make the baskets or get the rebounds. That's the way it is with us and God's laws. Sometimes we just say, "I want to do this instead, because I think it would be fun," even though we're not supposed to. Or we say, "I'm trying, but I just fail — I can't do it."

That's the way it is with this requirement of righteousness. We can't do it. The apostle Paul brought that out when he said:

> It is sin living in me, for I know that good itself does not dwell in me, that is, in my sinful nature. For I have the desire to do what is good, but I cannot carry it out. For I do not do the good I want to do, but the evil I do not want to do, this I keep on doing. What a wretched man I am! Who will rescue me from this body that is subject to death? Thanks be to God, who delivers me through Jesus Christ our Lord!

And there's the good news. Though I can't keep the requirement of those laws, Jesus did for me, in every way — with actions, words, thoughts, desires. He was sinless, and now that righteousness he gives to me. Jesus pointed this out at the beginning of his ministry:

> Do not think that I have come to abolish the Law or the Prophets; I have not come to abolish them but to fulfill them.

Because he knew we needed it and couldn't do it, that's why he came — to save us, to live for us.

One day a man ran up to Jesus, fell on his knees, and said, "Good teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?" Listen to Jesus' response:

> Why do you call me good? No one is good except God alone.

Now Jesus wasn't denying that he was good, or that he's God — in fact, that's exactly what he was emphasizing: "I am the good teacher because I am holy and righteous." Then he went on to point out the man's requirements and faults:

> You know the commandments: You shall not murder, you shall not commit adultery, you shall not steal, you shall not give false testimony, you shall not defraud, honor your father and mother.

With that short list of commandments, Jesus was pointing out what Martin Luther pointed out in his explanation in the Catechism to all of those commandments — there are things we are not supposed to do, and there are things we are required to do. We can't do it, but Jesus did it perfectly, and that righteousness I need has been received. God has told me about it; he's declared it in the gospel. Listen to how Paul explains it:

> Therefore no one will be declared righteous in God's sight by the works of the law; rather, through the law we become conscious of our sin. But now, apart from the law, the righteousness of God has been made known, to which the Law and the Prophets testify. This righteousness is given through faith in Jesus Christ to all who believe.

We have the righteousness that's required, through Jesus. God has declared it — that's how it is. Now, how do we get it? It was credited to us through faith. Paul goes on to explain:

> This is why "it was credited to him as righteousness." The words "it was credited to him" were written not for him alone, but also for us, to whom God will credit righteousness — for us who believe in him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead. He was delivered over to death for our sins and was raised to life for our justification.

I believe — believing in who Jesus is and what he has done: that he lived that perfect life, that he died the death that should have been ours because of our guilt against the commandments of God, and because of his resurrection from the dead, death has been removed, because the penalty had been paid by him. You and I are right with God. That's something we can say is true for us right now, as it will be forever. God has clothed us with that righteousness for eternity.

We might wonder, how did I get it? How did I become clothed with Christ's righteousness? Listen as Paul explains:

> All of you who were baptized into Christ have clothed yourselves with Christ.

That wonderful sacrament of baptism — when that water is put on us with God's word, our sins are removed, and God clothes us in that perfect righteousness of Jesus. So right now, God views us all as perfectly righteous, and that will last for eternity.

The apostle John, when he had a vision of the people of God in heaven, said this about them:

> She — that is, the bride of Christ, the Church of believers — was given bright, clean, fine linen to wear.

In fact, the fine linen is the "not guilty" verdict pronounced on the saints. God forever sees us as righteous.

Did I wow you? We are righteous in God's eyes forever. Now we might say "oh my goodness" as an expression of surprise, but now we can also say it as an expression of our need, and of the truth that we have goodness from God. The prophet Isaiah said it this way:

> I delight greatly in the Lord; my soul rejoices in my God. For he has clothed me with garments of salvation and arrayed me in a robe of his righteousness, as a bridegroom adorns his head like a priest, and as a bride adorns herself with her jewels.

Isaiah was wowed, knowing he has the garment of righteousness to wear. So no matter what you're going through, remember: perfect standing with God, forever. You are good — forever.

Let me take you back a few days. Remember Monday morning? Were any of you surprised by what was going on? It was raining in July. That morning I came out here to lead the 6:30 Bible class with the men, and there in the sky was this bright rainbow. I had to snatch a picture of it. Then, as I walked to the other side of the campus to open the gates so the guys could get in, there was a double rainbow — you can see it right above that tree on the right side, faintly.

Remember the message of the rainbow from God? He put it in the sky after Noah's flood to remind us of his forever grace. Sometimes there are clouds in our life — surprises, things that disappoint us — but there's always that blue sky in the distance. There's always God's rainbow above, no matter what, because we are always under the grace of God, since we are right with him through Jesus.

### 3. Living Out the Fruit of Goodness

Now let's talk about that fruit of goodness in our life. Listen to this lesson from Jesus:

> Make a tree good and its fruit will be good, or make a tree bad and its fruit will be bad, for a tree is recognized by its fruit... You brood of vipers, how can you who are evil say anything good? For the mouth speaks what the heart is full of. A good man brings good things out of the good stored up in him, and an evil man brings evil things out of the evil stored up in him.

— he was talking to those Pharisees. Make your tree good, so you grow good fruit. That's what God is doing in us.

So let's look at how we exercise that goodness in our life. The apostle Paul, before he gave us that list of fruits, said this:

> So I say, walk by the Spirit, and you will not gratify the desires of the flesh. For the flesh desires what is contrary to the Spirit, and the Spirit what is contrary to the flesh. They are in conflict with each other, so that you are not to do whatever you want.

Paul was describing an ongoing conflict — I call it a spiritual tug-of-war. My sin nature wants to pull me away from doing God's will, but the Spirit pulls me, with God's grace, to do what God wants me to do. So goodness starts with his goodness in me, and helps me restrain my sin nature — to say no to sin, not simply give in to it. So Paul says:

> Those who belong to Christ Jesus have crucified the flesh with its passions and desires. Since we live by the Spirit, let us keep in step with the Spirit.

Exercise your goodness with the Spirit working in you. Paul then gives us some examples:

> Let us not become weary in doing good, for at the proper time we will reap a harvest if we do not give up. Therefore, as we have opportunity, let us do good to all people, especially to those who belong to the family of believers.

Knowing that goodness of God, that righteousness of Christ, lives in me, I live that way to reflect the righteousness of Christ. To those who need help, give help. To those who need love, give love. To those who need compassion or encouragement, give them what they need — just as Christ would.

Peter gives this encouragement:

> Dear friends, I urge you, as foreigners and exiles, to abstain from sinful desires, which wage war against your soul. Live such good lives among the pagans that, though they accuse you of doing wrong, they may see your good deeds and glorify God on the day he visits us.

We live that good life not so people say "oh, they're such nice people" — not to our credit — but so that it rebounds to the glory of God, who gave us that goodness.

Oh my goodness! I want to explain it, I want to show it, I want to exercise it — the goodness that God has given me. Are you wowed? You should be. Look what God has done for you — he's made us good. Let's live it. Amen.

One way that we also reflect that goodness of God is by sharing that goodness with others. We do that with our offerings, which enable us to carry on the ministry here in our community and throughout the world. Let's ask God to bless our offerings.

> You did not spare your only Son, but gave him for a world undone; and freely with that blessed one, thou givest all. Whatever, Lord, we lend to thee, repaid a thousandfold will be. So gladly do we give to thee, who givest all. Amen.
